Pieces and notation

  • 1Tier-1 orb (amethyst). Fuses with another Tier-1.
  • 2Tier-2 orb (ruby). Made by fusing. Fills goals.
  • 2GOAL. Takes one Tier-2 orb.
  • ✓Filled goal. Stays put; blocks its lines.
  • Anchor (brass brackets). A cell property; can hold an orb.
  • Foldable line (dashed). Its label turns gold, e.g. R1.

Cells are written [row, col], counted from 0 at the top left. Moves are written R1 (fold row 1), C3 (fold column 3) and SNIP. A closed seam is drawn as a gold thread; an orb waiting on it sits on the thread.

The rules

These are the complete rules. The engine in seam/engine.mjs implements exactly this list and nothing else; if the two ever disagree, fix whichever is wrong and rebuild this page.

Folding

  1. A row or column can fold only if it is completely empty (no orbs, goals, filled goals or Anchor cells) and is not on the edge. Lines empty out during play as orbs fuse away, so new folds can open up.
  2. Only one seam can be closed at a time. While a seam is closed, the only legal move is SNIP.
  3. Folding line i makes the cells on lines i−1 and i+1 face each other, pair by pair. Only facing cells interact. Nothing slides. Every pair on the seam reacts at the same moment.

Reactions

  1. Tier-1 + Tier-1 fuse into one Tier-2 orb, which waits on the seam until it is snipped.
  2. Tier-2 + GOAL: the orb drops into the goal and fills it, immediately.
  3. Every other pair does nothing: Tier-1 + GOAL, Tier-1 + Tier-2, Tier-2 + Tier-2, anything + empty. There is no Tier-3.

Snipping

  1. SNIP reopens the seam. Each waiting Tier-2 lands in whichever of its two origin cells is an Anchor. If neither is, or both are, it lands in the top-most / left-most origin cell. Every other piece is exactly where it was.

Winning and scoring

  1. The level is won the moment every GOAL is filled. No snip is needed after the winning fold. There is no losing; a dead end is fixed with Undo or Reset.
  2. Every fold and every snip counts as one move, including undone ones. The score compares moves with the level's par: 1000 − time penalty − 100 × moves over par − 150 × hints, never below 0.

The trap

R3 also fuses orbs on the first move, so it looks like progress. It pairs the wrong orbs and the level can no longer be won.

→→
First moveWhat happensVerdict
R1fuses [0,2] + [2,2]Optimal
R3fuses [2,2] + [4,2]Dead end
C1nothing reactsDetour +2
C3fuses [4,2] + [4,4]Detour +2

Why 5×5 and not 4×4. We planned a 4×4 Master. Under these rules the solver found that a 4×4 board can't be longer than 3 moves: across 3 million random layouts, every solvable one had par 3. Every fold needs a whole empty inner line, and 4×4 has only four inner lines. 5×5 gives par-5 and par-7 levels.

FieldTypeMeaning

namestringShown to players.
difficultystandard | masterWhich tab it appears under.
size[rows, cols]Each 3–6.
orbs[{at: [r,c], tier: 1|2}]Starting orbs. One piece per cell.
anchors[[r,c]]Anchor cells. May sit under an orb or on an empty cell, never on a goal.
goals[[r,c]]At least one. Put them on the border: a goal on an inner line blocks that line forever.
parnumberFewest moves. The checker fails if this disagrees with the solver.
solution["R1", "SNIP", …]One optimal solution. The checker replays it.

Tools

All plain Node.js (18+), no dependencies. Run from seam/.

FileWhat it does
engine.mjsThe rules, as pure functions: legalMoves, applyMove, isWon, and solve, which searches every reachable position (Daily #2 has 41). The game should import this file instead of reimplementing the rules.
check.mjsValidates a level file. Errors: bad coordinates, overlaps, unsolvable, wrong par, solution that doesn't replay. Warnings: Anchors that never matter, more than 3 optimal solutions, two Anchors that can fuse. Prints every opening as optimal, detour or dead end. Exits 1 on errors, so it can run in CI.
design.mjsSearches random layouts for good levels: par in range, at most 2 optimal solutions, Anchors that matter, and a tempting opening that fuses but is a detour or dead end. --size 5 --tries 600000 --seed 5 --min-par 5 --max-par 9
spec/build-spec.mjsRebuilds this page from the engine and levels.json.

node check.mjs

Screen and scoring

ElementBehavior
Instruction“Fold empty rows and columns to bring matching orbs together and fill every goal.”
FoldingTap anywhere on a dashed line (the whole row or column is the tap target, at least 44px tall). Tap the gold thread to snip.
Move counter“Moves 4 · Par 5” under the board. Replaces Crossfire's check counter.
UndoPrimary button. Steps back one move; the counter still goes up.
ResetBack to the start. Moves and time keep counting.
Stuck noticeWhen the solver finds no way to win from the current position: “No way forward from here. Undo or reset.”
HintAfter the ad, plays the solver's best next move from the current position, including after wrong moves.
QA keyDevelopment builds only: shows the solution and an auto-solve button.
AnimationSimple slide, about 250ms: the folded line shrinks into the gold thread and its neighbors slide together; a fusion pulses and the number changes. Papercraft folding is a later polish pass.
ResultsTime, moves vs par, hints, score, share grid (one square per move: fold, snip, or a fold that reacted).

Open questions

  • Daily generation. design.mjs finds a few good 5×5 levels per 600,000 tries. Enough for curated levels, not yet for a daily feed without a human picking.
  • Tier-3 orbs. Excluded for now. Adding them means new reactions and a new goal type.
  • Move limit. We chose par with no fail state. If testing shows players don't care about par, a move limit is the fallback.
  • Papercraft animation. Deferred until the rules are proven in play.
